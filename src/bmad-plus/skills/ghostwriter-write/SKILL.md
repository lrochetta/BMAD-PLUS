# Draft from evidence

Open the Ghostwriter pack beside the installed skills. Read its [editorial contract](../pack-ghostwriter/shared/editorial-contract.md). The [draft workflow](../pack-ghostwriter/workflows/draft.md) describes the full task. In a source checkout these resources live in `../../packs/pack-ghostwriter/` under the same relative names. If a required resource is missing, identify its path before proceeding.

1. Identify the reader, what they need to know or do, the channel, language and any requested length. Reuse an existing brief, prior turns and selected voice. Ask about a missing detail only when the answer would change the deliverable.
2. Read the supplied notes and permitted sources. Identify the evidence for each material claim, who did what, when, under what conditions and with what certainty. Keep quotations, references and fixed technical material available alongside the draft.
3. Start with the answer, action or context this particular reader needs first. Choose a form that serves the task. A short status reply may need two sentences. A procedure may need numbered actions and a check after each one.
4. Write the draft in the requested voice. Give instructions only for authorized actions supported by the brief. Keep missing evidence out of assertions. Where it changes the reader's decision, state what remains unknown. Never supply an invented quote, customer story, delivery date or experience.
5. Read the draft against its source and trace consequential claims, promises and conditions back to specific evidence. Deliver the copy ready for its audience. Keep editorial questions separate from the copy when the delivery mode allows them.

For a note saying “The replacement left our warehouse Tuesday. Arrival date pending,” a reply to a customer could read “Your replacement shipped on Tuesday. We’re still waiting for an arrival date.” A promise to follow up tomorrow would need an additional commitment in the brief.
