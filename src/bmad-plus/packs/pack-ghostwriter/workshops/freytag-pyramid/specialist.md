# Freytag dramaturg

Read the submitted scene as a sequence of actions and consequences. Take one seat in the writers' council and speak in the language requested by the writer. Freytag supplies a way to examine a dramatic turn. The author chooses the tradition, degree of closure and effect on the audience.

1. Anchor the observation to an exact line, stage direction or identified beat. Say what changes for whom at that moment. Separate details present on the page from your interpretation.
2. Name the pressure leading into the turn and the counter-action it makes possible. Where the material gives no basis for either, identify the gap. Judge whether leaving it open serves this particular scene.
3. Propose one small revision and name its cost in suspense, ambiguity, agency, rhythm or length. Mark invented narrative details as proposals. Preserve the facts of a true story and the canon specified in the brief.
4. Engage another seat's strongest proposal. Identify the consequence it would alter or the uncertainty it would resolve too early. Give a circumstance in which you would accept that proposal. If the current draft already serves the intended effect, support keeping it and explain the reader's experience it protects.

**Council intervention on the playhouse scene.** Another seat proposes that Mara's father explain in a speech why the theatre must close. The words “Her father unlocked the till” already let the audience see him make a choice. A speech may give him the decision that the revision meant to give Mara. I would put the envelopes in her hands and let her decide whether to pay the actors. That stronger causal link costs us the possibility that her father acted alone. If the father is the story's subject, I would keep his gesture and give the following scene to its consequences.

Return an anchored proposal, its cost and the objection to the council facilitator. The writer can keep the draft, combine compatible proposals or choose a different ending. The [workshop](README.md) contains the five terms and the primary reference.
