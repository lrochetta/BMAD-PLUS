# Freytag's pyramid

Use this workshop when a story builds pressure but the writer cannot tell what turns the action or why the ending follows. Bring the scene or outline, the intended effect on the audience, and any continuity constraints. During a council, seat the [dramaturg](specialist.md) alongside the other readers of the same draft.

Gustav Freytag described a pyramidal structure in *Die Technik des Dramas*, first published in 1863. In chapter II of Elias J. MacEwan's English translation, the ascending action meets a high point and the following action develops its consequences. His fifth part is the **catastrophe**, the closing action in his account of drama. Some contemporary teaching diagrams end with **resolution** or **dénouement**. Those are useful terms for a broader exercise, including a comedy or an open ending. Identify which interpretation the workshop is using. Use the practical vocabulary below by default. In a historical reading, label the first part *introduction*, the fourth *return or fall* and the last *catastrophe*, following MacEwan.

The diagram describes relations among actions. Its slope gives no page count, chapter length or minimum number of reversals. A single irreversible decision can appear in a quiet scene. When the material belongs to another dramatic tradition, unfolds in several strands, or is a short nonfiction account, use only the questions that illuminate the writer's aim.

## Try it on one scene

**Shared, fictional draft.** The town's only playhouse is about to close. “Mara laid the actors’ unpaid envelopes on the ticket counter. Her father unlocked the till. By morning the theatre was dark.”

1. Name the missing connection. Unlocking the till offers a way to pay the cast. The final image alone leaves us unsure why the theatre closed.
2. Propose one playable choice. A possible revision has Mara hand the contents of the till to the cast and keep the empty till open in view. The creditors arriving next morning are a **proposed addition** requiring the author's approval. This choice gives the empty building a cause.
3. Offer a version at a different cost. Keep the creditors offstage and end on the empty till. It preserves the draft's restraint but leaves the fate of the building unresolved. If uncertainty is the desired ending, the original may already work.
4. Ask the writer which consequence matters. In a writing workshop, alternatives remain proposals until the author chooses one. For a factual account, ask what happened and retain the gap while it is unknown.

## Ask in the language of the room

- **FR** — « Où se joue la décision qui change le sens de la dernière image ? Montre-moi le passage et propose une autre lecture qui laisserait la fin ouverte. »
- **EN** — “Which choice makes the final image follow? Point to the moment in the draft and show what we would lose by making the connection explicit.”
- **PT-BR** — “Em que momento a decisão da personagem muda o rumo da cena? Mostre o trecho e sugira uma alternativa que preserve a dúvida do final.”
