# The Story Spine · L’épine dorsale de l’histoire · Espinha dorsal da história

Use this exercise when the draft contains interesting incidents and the writer cannot say why one leads to the next. Kenn Adams dates his eight-line improvisation exercise to 1991. He describes the original structure and credits fellow improviser Kat Koppett with its name. Pixar later helped popularize it and teaches it in *Pixar in a Box*. The example below is original. [Adams, Back to the Story Spine](https://www.aerogrammestudio.com/2013/06/05/back-to-the-story-spine/) · [Adams on the name](https://www.aerogrammestudio.com/2013/03/22/the-story-spine-pixars-4th-rule-of-storytelling/) · [Pixar and Khan Academy, Story structure](https://www.khanacademy.org/pixar)

## Eight links

Write one sentence per row in the language of the room. In rows 4 to 6, join the sentences aloud with **« Et c’est pourquoi… »**. In English, try “As a result…” and in Brazilian Portuguese, “Por isso…”. Remove the prompts when writing the finished scene. If a consequence still makes sense after removing its supposed cause, investigate that link.

| Link | Work the sentence does | Fictional example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Situation | Place a person in a world with something at stake. | Mara sold tickets in her father's playhouse. |
| 2. Routine | Say what normally happens and who keeps it going. | Each night she reserved the next lighting payment before dividing the takings among the actors. |
| 3. Disruption | Change something the routine cannot absorb. | One night the till held enough for the lighting payment or for wages. |
| 4. First consequence | Show how a person responds to the broken routine. | Mara handed every coin to the actors. |
| 5. Second consequence | Trace something made costly or possible by that choice. | The supplier collected the unpaid lamps. |
| 6. Third consequence | Follow the new constraint through someone's action. | Unable to rehearse indoors after dark, the cast carried its stools onto the pavement. |
| 7. Decisive result | Locate the action at which the central question changes. | A crowd gathered for the rehearsal, and Mara opened the performance there. |
| 8. New equilibrium | Show a repeatable difference in everyday life. | On show nights the ticket desk stood outside with the actors. |

**Try the weak link.** Moving the stools explains why an audience could see the cast. It does not guarantee that anyone came. If an audience gathering by chance feels too convenient, let an actor invite the people already waiting at the door. This addition requires the author's approval. An empty pavement would produce a different, equally playable ending.

**For a documented event**, two occurrences in sequence may have independent causes. Mark the relationship as unknown unless the source supports it. For a dream sequence or deliberately episodic piece, retain the writer's discontinuity when that is part of its effect.

- **FR** — « Remonte de la dernière image vers la première. À quel endroit “Et c’est pourquoi…” ne tient-il plus ? »
- **EN** — “Which later event would still happen if we removed this choice?”
- **PT-BR** — “Se retirarmos essa decisão, qual acontecimento ainda faria sentido? Que mudança aparece na rotina do final?”
