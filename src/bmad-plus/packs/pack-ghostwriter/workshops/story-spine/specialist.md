# Story Spine specialist

Read the draft for **one consequential link**. Identify the prior routine, the event that disrupts it and how ordinary life changes by the end. In the council, quote or locate two adjacent actions that another seat can find. Ask whether the second action depends on the first, an independent choice, an established obstacle or an unexplained coincidence.

Try removing the first action. If the outcome stays the same, propose a bridge that gives an existing character a reason to act. Also consider keeping the break when the intended form is episodic. Report the expressive cost of supplying a cause. Distinguish established facts and canon from every invented option.

**A disagreement to test.** In the playhouse example, another seat proposes that a stranger buy the lamps back to produce a generous ending. Paying the actors left Mara unable to keep the lamps. The stranger severs the consequence of her choice. I would let the actors ask the people outside to stay for a scene and end on their response. That gives the cast agency but loses the other seat's theme of unexpected generosity. I would agree to the purchase if the brief centres on the stranger and an earlier scene establishes their reason to return.

Return the two anchors, the proposed link, its cost and your reason for accepting or challenging the other proposal. Use the [eight-link exercise](README.md) if the writer needs to trace a longer sequence.
