# Dan Harmon’s story circle

Use **Le cercle d’histoire de Dan Harmon**, **Dan Harmon’s story circle** or **O círculo de histórias de Dan Harmon** to examine a pursuit from the character’s usual routine through its price and back home. The specialist can read an episode, subplot or single comic scene. Give each circle a subject and desire. In an ensemble, trace one person at a time or specify whose shared objective the group pursues.

## Eight questions for a draft

| Step | Function · FR / EN | What the reader can look for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Confort / Familiar routine | Which small action shows what this character can usually count on? |
| 2 | Désir / Want | What particular thing would they cross the room to obtain today? |
| 3 | Inconnu / Unfamiliar situation | Which decision puts their ordinary way of getting it out of reach? |
| 4 | Adaptation / Adjustment | What new rule do they learn, borrow or break to make progress? |
| 5 | Obtention / Gain | Where can the reader see that the character has the thing they sought? |
| 6 | Prix / Price | Which consequence comes specifically from getting it, and who bears it? |
| 7 | Retour / Return | Which familiar obligation, person or prop can the character meet again? |
| 8 | Changement / Change | What do they do differently on that encounter? What remains unresolved? |

Harmon presented the eight movements in his Channel 101 writing lessons. If several movements share a scene, give them the same anchor. For an extract, distinguish the edge of the supplied text from a missing beat in the complete story. [Dan Harmon’s original lesson, preserved by the Channel 101 Wiki](https://channel101.fandom.com/wiki/Story_Structure_101:_Super_Basic_Shit)

## A disagreement about timing

In this invented radio comedy, Nara sweeps up after the studio audience every Friday. She wants to introduce the band. The presenter agrees to swap jobs for one night. Nara learns to follow the producer’s hand signals and gets her first introduction exactly right. In the closing shot, she spots the presenter kneeling under the seats, picking up the paper stars Nara fired from a confetti tube.

A Story Circle reading would cut from the falling stars straight to the presenter’s knees in the aisle. The hard cut makes someone else pay for Nara’s flourish while the audience still remembers it. A Hero’s Journey reading might instead spend the last few seconds on Nara putting down the microphone and taking the next row beside him. That action shows a changed relationship, while the extra beat lets the laugh die down. The author can try both endings against the running time and decide whose gesture should carry the scene.

## Convene a reading

Send the [specialist card](specialist.md) with the scene numbering, the desired tone and any ending the author has fixed. For a question about transformation, invite [The hero’s journey](../hero-journey/README.md) as a second seat. Notes from a single agent can support a solo revision. Count only independently returned contributions when reporting a council debate.
