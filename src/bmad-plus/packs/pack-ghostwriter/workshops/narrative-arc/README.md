# Narrative arc · Arc narratif général · Arco narrativo

Choose this reading when individual scenes work but the writer is unsure what holds the story together. Follow one expectation across the whole piece. Identify what matters to the audience, which scenes complicate it, where its meaning changes, and what the ending allows the audience to conclude. Nonchronological, quiet and open-ended stories can sustain that movement. [OpenStax, *Writing Guide with Handbook*, discussion of narrative](https://openstax.org/books/writing-guide/pages/3-3-glance-at-genre-the-literacy-narrative)

The four rows are questions for reading the entire work. A scene can serve several functions and parallel storylines can reach their turning points in different chapters. Ask the [Freytag specialist](../freytag-pyramid/README.md) when the writer specifically wants a five-part progression and the consequences after a central turn.

## An original example

Suppose a story opens with a child painting out the word *Reserved* on a theatre seat. Only later do we learn that her mother has kept that seat untouched at every performance since her father's death. Before we know the ritual, the painted sign reads as mischief. Afterwards, the same act carries a question about who gets to decide how the family remembers him. When the mother sits in the repainted seat, the gesture can resolve the conflict without settling whether either person feels ready to talk about the loss.

Map the moments in the order the audience encounters them. Write down what the reader believes after each. Try moving the revelation about the seat to the opening. The child may appear more deliberate, but the audience loses its chance to revise a mistaken judgement. Either order can serve a different brief.

- **FR** — « Quelle question le lecteur se pose-t-il au début, puis au moment où la mère s’assoit ? Qu’est-ce qui peut rester ouvert ? »
- **EN** — “What changes in the reader's understanding between the opening image and its return?”
- **PT-BR** — “O que a pessoa que lê espera no início? Qual cena muda essa expectativa e o que o desfecho deixa em aberto?”
