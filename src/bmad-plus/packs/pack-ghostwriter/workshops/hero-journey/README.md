# The hero’s journey

An optional Ghostwriter workshop for an author who wants to examine a character’s transformation. Invoke it as **Le voyage du héros**, **The hero’s journey** or **A jornada do herói**. Give the specialist a scene, its place in the story and any outcome the author wants to preserve. A solo reading can start with an excerpt. A council debate needs the same excerpt for all its participants.

## Three questions for the manuscript

| Movement | A question the writer can answer on the page |
| --- | --- |
| Departure · départ · partida | What has the character relied on up to now? Which choice puts that habit, relationship or privilege at risk? |
| Initiation · initiation · iniciação | Which costly choice exposes the limits of the old habit? What does the character actually do when another person needs them? |
| Return · retour · retorno | Who encounters the consequence after the ordeal? What action lets the reader recognize a change, a refusal to change or a loss? |

Joseph Campbell describes separation, initiation and return in his account of the monomyth. His examples vary within those movements. Christopher Vogler adapts the idea into a twelve-stage guide for screenwriters. Use either vocabulary when it helps an author resolve a specific problem. A missing mentor, quest or homecoming can be entirely appropriate to the manuscript. [Joseph Campbell Foundation](https://www.jcf.org/learn/joseph-campbell-heros-journey) · [Christopher Vogler’s practical guide](https://mf.media.mit.edu/courses/2006/mas845/readings/files/Writers%20Journey.pdf)

## The twelve-stage reading

For a request about the twelve stages, follow Vogler’s screenwriting version. Start from the protagonist’s familiar world, trace their venture into the unknown and examine what changes on their return. Locate the stages the author has actually written. A passage can serve two functions. A deliberately omitted or reordered beat can be a sound choice. [Christopher Vogler’s practical guide](https://mf.media.mit.edu/courses/2006/mas845/readings/files/Writers%20Journey.pdf)

| Stage | French / English | Editorial question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Monde ordinaire / Familiar world | Which ordinary action establishes how the character gets through a day? |
| 2 | Appel de l’aventure / An invitation to leave | What disturbance makes staying put a consequential choice? |
| 3 | Refus de l’appel / Reluctance | What could they lose by saying yes, and where can we see them hesitate? |
| 4 | Rencontre avec le mentor / Guidance | Who or what offers help, and which decision still belongs to the protagonist? |
| 5 | Franchissement du premier seuil / First crossing | Which action commits them to life under unfamiliar rules? |
| 6 | Épreuves, alliés et ennemis / Tests and new allegiances | Which test changes whom they trust and shows why? |
| 7 | Approche de la caverne / Approach to danger | What must they prepare to face, and what do they still misunderstand? |
| 8 | Épreuve décisive / Central ordeal | What choice carries a cost they can no longer avoid? |
| 9 | Récompense / What is won | What do they gain, and what can that gain still fail to repair? |
| 10 | Chemin du retour / The way home | What unfinished obligation follows them back? |
| 11 | Résurrection / A final test of change | When the old habit would be easiest, what do they do this time? |
| 12 | Retour avec l’élixir / Bringing the gain home | Who can now act differently because the protagonist returned transformed? |

## A choice about an ending

An invented story opens with Awa pocketing the chalk after each night shift at the tram depot. She alone decides which faults to leave off the board. One night she reports her own mistake, knowing it could cost her the shift. A second driver countersigns. The proposed ending reads, “The inspector unlocked the gate. The first tram rolled out.”

The Three Acts specialist could end on the tram. Opening the gate pays off the external question of whether the line can reopen. The Hero’s Journey specialist could add one silent action. Awa sets the chalk on the ledge within the second driver’s reach. The gesture lets a reader see who can speak at the depot tomorrow. It also takes the last image away from the tram. If the author wants a brisk procedural ending, the first cut serves that purpose. If the story has promised a change in how the drivers share responsibility, the second ending gives that change a material consequence. Both are proposals for the author.

## Using the specialist

Delegate the [specialist card](specialist.md) with the same scene anchors, story constraints and editorial question sent to the other seats. For disagreement about pacing or payoff, pair it with [The three acts](../three-act/README.md). Mark an absent participant as missing. A host working alone can present a single reading and invite the author to decide.

For a mosaic of witness accounts whose viewpoints purposely stay irreconcilable, the specialist can identify the formal intention and recommend keeping it. On factual material, preserve the subjects’ own accounts and avoid attributing an inner transformation without evidence.
